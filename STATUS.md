# Our question is whether companies that rely more on imports or on China, based on their own 10-K filings, dropped more after the April 2, 2025 tariff announcement. We think it’s a good test because the 10-Ks were written before the announcement, so our exposure measure can’t be influenced by anything that happened after. That matters, because otherwise it would be easy to “find” exposure in companies we already know fell. Our team started with the Fed idea, but we switched to this one because it gave us a cleaner before-and-after setup. My part so far has been building the dashboard and the “data contract,” which is the spec for what the final data needs to look like. 

# So far, we tested the full process on three real companies: Apple scored a 4 and fell 5.75 points relative to the market over April 3–4, Nike also scored a 4 but only fell 0.65 points, and Verizon scored a 2 and actually rose 7.07 points. The interesting part to me is that Apple and Nike got the same score but moved very differently. My guess is that our score treats all import exposure the same, when where you import from matters: Apple’s supply chain runs mostly through China, while Nike’s runs mostly through Vietnam and Indonesia, and on April 4 China retaliated while Vietnam signaled it wanted to negotiate. In full honesty, a lot is still unfinished; the scores came from an AI and hasn’t been checked by a person yet, and we still don’t have the beta-adjusted return. Something also broke along the way, since the dashboard was built for 500 companies and had to be adjusted to work with just 3. The next step is running Carter’s script, which can now handle all ~500 companies but hasn’t been ran yet. 

# We have three questions we would like answered. First, is hiding company names from the AI enough to keep it from using what it “knows” about April 2025, or should we use a model whose training data ends before then? Masking is our current plan, but a model can probably still recognize Apple from it’s 10-K, and this feels like the biggest risk to the project. Second, we’re using SPY’s holdings from March 31 as our S&P 500 list for April 2; is that close enough to avoid survivorship bias, or do we need the exact list from April 2? Lastly, since China announced retaliatory tariffs on April 4, inside our April 3–4 window, should we also report April 3 on its own, or keep the two-day window and just disclose it?
